“Job titles mean nothing when survival takes over”

Getting stranded with your boss sounds like a nightmare before survival even enters the picture. Send Help takes that setup and finds something deliciously ugly inside it: the person used to calling the shots may have very little to offer when the office disappears.

There’s a satisfying tension in watching workplace authority meet a situation it can’t control. A title, money, and confidence only carry so much weight when staying alive depends on actual ability. Suddenly, being overlooked and being useless are two very different things.

That’s where the premise gets interesting. Two people need each other, but they also bring resentment, ego, and a history that doesn’t vanish just because they share a disaster. Cooperation sounds sensible. Trust is a much bigger ask.

The dark humor gives that conflict a nasty little edge. There’s room to enjoy the absurdity while still thinking about who holds power, who deserves it, and what happens when the balance changes. Surviving the island is one problem. Surviving each other is another.

This was a banger for both of us. A survival thriller with an ugly human problem at its center, and the kind of setup that makes “just work together” sound a whole lot easier than it is. An easy Wave recommendation.
