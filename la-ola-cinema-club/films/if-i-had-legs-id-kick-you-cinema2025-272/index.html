A haunting mosaic of pain, choices, and impossible questions.

If I Had Legs, I’d Kick You sits in the surreal-drama lane where you’re meant to feel more than you understand. It moves through discomfort, ambiguity, and small human disasters in a way that mirrors how life collapses when too many fractures stack up. The film blends grounded struggle with dreamlike uncertainty, pulling you into a world where nothing is neatly explained and every interpretation feels both right and wrong at the same time.

It watches like a stream of tense moments where people make decisions out of fear, confusion, exhaustion, and survival. You see a mother spiraling under pressure, a daughter fading in the background, strangers appearing with unclear intentions, and everyone slipping between clarity and delusion. The movie never tells you which version of events is true. Instead, it lets you sit in the discomfort of piecing together what you think is happening without ever confirming whether you’re right.

There is a real sadness underneath everything — the kind that comes from watching someone try their best in impossible situations, yet still fail to protect the people who depend on them. The film brings up questions about responsibility, trauma, neglect, hope, guilt, and how easily life can unravel when someone is drowning emotionally with nobody around them to pull them out. You feel the weight of choices, the weight of avoidance, the weight of love twisted by circumstance.

What makes the film wild is how much it leaves unanswered. You never fully know who to trust, what is hallucination versus reality, what certain characters symbolize, or what the final moments truly mean. It leaves you holding the pieces, deciding for yourself whether the ending is hopeful, tragic, symbolic, literal, or something in between. It forces you to confront the idea that life rarely gives clean explanations, and sometimes the story only exists to stir the questions inside you.

The acting sells it completely. You believe the world, even if you don’t understand it. And the final sequence lands with a mix of beauty and confusion that leaves you staring at the screen after the credits.

From the Brain

Strong acting, heavy themes, unclear by design but impactful.

From the Heart

Emotional, haunting, and unsettling in a strangely human way.
