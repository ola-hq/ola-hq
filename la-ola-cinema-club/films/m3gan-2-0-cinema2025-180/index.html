When the joke runs too long, even robots get rusty.

The first M3GAN had that viral mix of campy horror, sly satire, and TikTok-friendly menace. The sequel tries to bottle that lightning again, but instead of feeling fresh, it leans harder into winks and overexplanations. Horror sequels always face the same trap — go bigger, go sillier, or go scarier — and M3GAN 2.0 seems unsure which lane it actually wants.

The result? A movie that feels like it’s trying way too hard. The humor gets forced, the “tech paranoia” angle gets shoved down your throat, and the story drifts into full fantasy territory without the bite of the first one. It’s not unwatchable — M3GAN is still fun to spend time with, and there are some chaotic sequences that’ll make the theater laugh — but it never really scares, and it definitely doesn’t surprise. Think less “AI nightmare fuel,” more “action-doll chaos with a few punchlines.”
