A B-list movie wrapped in an A-list cast.

This one felt like an indie flick dressed up with stars — Sydney Sweeney, Paul Walter Hauser, and a handful of familiar faces anchoring a story that constantly made you ask what the fuck is going on? Between the kid who thinks he’s Native American, the complicated mother figure, and the mix of tribal politics and personal messes, there were always multiple moving parts to track.

It wasn’t perfect, but it was never boring. The adventure format gave space for each character to stand out without anyone really outshining the others. The arcs landed well enough, especially the lead woman’s gradual evolution into claiming what was hers by the end. It had that “independent spirit with Hollywood polish” vibe that makes you unsure whether you’re watching prestige or pulp, but either way, it kept the ride entertaining.
