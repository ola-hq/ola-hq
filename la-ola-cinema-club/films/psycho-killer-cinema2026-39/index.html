“A twisted chase that makes for a good time”

Psycho Killer starts with a Kansas highway patrol officer tracking the man who murdered her husband. It gives the hunt a personal reason to matter, then takes it into the territory of a serial killer whose intentions stretch beyond that single crime. Grief and pursuit make a solid setup for some dark entertainment.

Twisted is the word that sticks. There’s a particular kind of enjoyment in following a story this disturbing: you’re interested in where it’s going even when the places it takes you aren’t exactly pleasant. The ugliness becomes part of the draw, giving the movie an edge that makes the experience entertaining.

That’s where Psycho Killer worked for us. It was a good flick to get caught up in, with enough of that warped energy to make the watch worthwhile. Enjoying a horror movie can mean enjoying the discomfort—the fascination of seeing just how far a story is willing to go.

A solid pick for a night when you want something dark and twisted. We had a good time with it, even if “a good time” looks a little messed up here.
