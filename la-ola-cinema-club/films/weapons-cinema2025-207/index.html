Dark, layered, and unrelenting — a story where nobody wins.

Weapons cements itself as one of those ambitious, interwoven narrative thrillers that dares to cross perspectives, blur morality, and challenge the audience to sit in discomfort. It’s not a franchise grab or a popcorn throwaway — it’s part of the wave of modern horror/thriller hybrids that push psychological unease and social commentary into the mainstream. With its layered structure, shifting POVs, and morally tangled core, it forces you to confront how messy humanity can get when survival, guilt, and fear clash.

From the opening shot — a faceless figure you somehow already recognize — the film grips you. The narration by a child outside the central events makes it even more haunting, setting a tone of innocence watching horror unfold. The story plays like a series of dominoes: one perspective ends in shock, the next picks up just a little further down the line, overlapping and reframing everything you thought you knew. That momentum keeps pushing until you’re knee-deep in chaos, watching a town turn against a girl while every side feels simultaneously justified and unforgivable.

It’s suffocating at times — fight after fight, twist after twist — but that relentlessness is intentional. The film is designed to exhaust you, to leave you in that “nobody really wins” headspace. And while it’s not the kind of ending you walk out of feeling good about, the craftsmanship is undeniable. The main actress carries it hard, her presence anchoring a story that could have easily collapsed under its own weight. Even when the pieces felt unexplainable, the sheer tension and artistry made it worth sticking through.

It’s not perfect, it’s not comforting, and it’s not an easy rewatch. But it’s unforgettable — a horror-thriller that digs under your skin and stays there.
