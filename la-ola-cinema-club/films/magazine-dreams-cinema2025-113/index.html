A heartbreaking pursuit of glory, where ambition eats you alive.

Magazine Dreams lands hard because it pulls back the curtain on obsessed ambition — the kind that wears you down even as you chase the spotlight. It matters because this isn’t just another success story: it’s about how far someone is willing to push themselves, body and mind, for a dream that might never return the love you pour into it. The film digs into body image, mental health, and the cost of being seen.

This one left me with a weird, heavy feeling. It was heartbreaking to watch — you root for him so much, but also hope he stops and can find help. The world of competitive bodybuilding is one we rarely get on screen, especially with this much internal chaos. After the credits, it stuck with me. But enjoyment? Not really. It was uncomfortable, more pain than uplift.

The film tried. Jonathan Majors gives a committed performance as Killian Maddox, and there are moments where vulnerability cuts through the discomfort. But the craft felt uneven — the storyline often sliding into pain without payoff, scenes that dragged, and emotional beats that teetered between raw and repetitive. It’s not a film you walk out feeling good, but it’s one you walk out feeling moved.
