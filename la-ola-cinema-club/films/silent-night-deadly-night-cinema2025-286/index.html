A cursed childhood turns Santa into something darker.

On the surface, this looks like just another holiday slasher — killer Santa, cheap shocks, seasonal gimmick. At first, it honestly feels like that too. You’re watching it thinking, okay, here we go, another Christmas kill movie, and expecting nothing more than surface-level carnage.

But as the story unfolds, it starts to shift. The movie quietly reframes itself into something more tragic and strange. Instead of just leaning into shock value, it builds a character who’s been dealt a genuinely brutal hand in life — trauma stacked on trauma — and tries, in his own warped way, to make sense of it. What begins as violence starts to feel like a curse he’s struggling to live with rather than pure evil.

That’s where the movie becomes unexpectedly compelling. You’re not just watching kills anymore — you’re watching someone trying to impose order, balance, and meaning onto a world that taught him cruelty early and often. The film leans into a twisted moral framework, almost like a grim fairy tale or a dark Robin Hood fantasy, where “punishment” is aimed at wrongdoing. It’s uncomfortable, but intentional.

By the end, you realize the movie pulled off something risky: it gets you rooting for the killer. Not because the violence is justified, but because the humanity underneath it is visible. That tension — between empathy and horror — is what makes the film stick longer than expected. What starts as dismissible ends up surprisingly engaging, and even emotionally grounded in its own warped logic.

It’s not perfect, and it’s not subtle — but it’s far more thoughtful than its reputation suggests. By the final act, it hooks you enough that you’re genuinely curious where the story goes next.
