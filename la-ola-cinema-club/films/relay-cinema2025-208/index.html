Layers on layers of deception, connection, and survival.

Relay slides into that niche of action-thrillers that are both slick and strangely grounded. It’s not just bullets and car chases — it leans into process, systems, and the way corruption trickles down to ordinary people who get caught in the crossfire. With its emphasis on middle-men, handoffs, and layers of secrecy, the film mirrors real-world business and political shadows, where accountability gets buried under endless intermediaries. It also deserves credit for weaving accessibility into its DNA, highlighting how communication tools (like relay interpreting for the deaf community) shift dynamics in tense, high-stakes situations.

Relay hooked us more than expected. The stylish cinematography, sharp sound design, and clever pacing gave it that “Bourne Identity but fresh” type of vibe. The whole conceit of middle-men calling on other middle-men was fascinating — a rabbit hole of power dynamics and misdirection where every time you thought the net had closed, it slipped wider open. The characters carried the weight, especially in those moments when naivety clashed with survival instincts. Yes, some choices felt unbelievably dumb, but that’s also what kept the tension high: watching someone stumble into disaster while you’re screaming, “don’t do it!”

There were definitely points where it got confusing, maybe because we missed the first few minutes, maybe because the film wanted us disoriented. Either way, it landed. The relay concept itself was eye-opening — whether factually airtight or not, it added texture to the story, and seeing it all come together was worth the ride. It’s not perfect, but it’s the kind of thriller that leaves you chewing over its structure, its “what ifs,” and its commentary on how messy the truth can get when nobody’s hands are clean.
