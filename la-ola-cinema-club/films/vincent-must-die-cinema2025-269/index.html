Vincent Must Die

Thriller

A bizarre, unsettling what-if dropped into an ordinary world.

Vincent Must End sits in that offbeat thriller-comedy lane where a surreal, almost absurd premise is dropped into a world that otherwise feels completely normal. The movie plays with that tension — something horrific is happening, yet everyone around the main character reacts with an odd mix of disbelief, apathy, and shrugging acceptance. It creates a strange atmosphere where you cannot tell if the film is making a cultural point, a tonal joke, or simply leaning into the weirdness of its own universe.

At its core, the movie follows a man who suddenly becomes the target of random violence from strangers, and no one seems fazed by it except him. People don’t believe him, or they brush it off, or they act like it’s not that serious. That disconnect is where most of the tension comes from. You keep thinking, in the U.S. this would be a whole different story — lawsuits, outrage, viral videos, people losing their minds. But in this French setting, everything feels strangely low-stakes in the reactions, which becomes both unsettling and unintentionally funny. You’re stuck wondering whether that’s cultural, stylistic, or the film’s commentary on isolation and disbelief.

Narratively, it stretches itself a little long (like this review.. cough cough). There are stretches where you feel them delaying the inevitable just to prolong the question of how it’s all going to end. And then the moment finally arrives… and the movie refuses to give you the payoff you spent the whole runtime expecting. It’s one of those films that seems entirely aware of what the audience wants and actively chooses not to deliver it. Not in a clever way — more in a “sorry, you’re not getting that” way.

There is something fascinating about the concept, though. The movie feels like a metaphor about being dismissed, ignored, or left alone to deal with a nightmare no one takes seriously. But it also has moments where the reactions are so casual they pull you out of the experience. You’re left unsure whether it’s intentional surrealism or just an odd creative choice.

It is interesting, weird, frustrating, and definitely memorable — but also uneven. You walk out saying, that was something, but I don’t know if it was good or if I just survived it.

From the Brain

Odd pacing, hollow payoff, concept stronger than execution.

From the Heart

Bizarre, tense, unique, and surprisingly fun to experience.
