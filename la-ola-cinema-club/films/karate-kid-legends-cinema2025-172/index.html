Wax on, wax off — nostalgia polished but never sharpened.

This latest entry in the Karate Kid legacy leans hard into the past. It pulls in Mr. Miyagi’s shadow, Daniel-san memories, and even bridges eras with Jackie Chan — a living legend who can’t help but elevate whatever he touches. There’s a new generation stepping up, plenty of callbacks for fans, and a few surprise cameos sprinkled in for good measure. It’s meant to be the torch-passing film, the “Legends” chapter that ties everything together.

And while that all sounds great, the truth is the movie never really kicks above “fine.” Nostalgia keeps it watchable, but the story itself is basic — training montages, rivalries, redemption arcs, rinse and repeat. It has moments that hit (some sweet, some crowd-pleasing), but they’re surrounded by predictability. If you grew up with the franchise, you’ll probably smile a few times. If you didn’t, this won’t be the movie to make you a fan. At the end of the day, it’s more wax than shine.
