Great actors, weird choices — a movie that misses its own note.

Every year there’s a film that feels like it should be a big deal — stacked cast, intriguing synopsis, artsy ambition — but ends up face-planting under the weight of its own hype. Opus lands right there. It matters less as a cultural milestone and more as a reminder that not every high-concept gamble pays off, no matter who’s on the poster.

Honestly, not much to say. Hard to watch, harder to follow, and I was definitely dozing off at points. It had good actors, sure, but the whole thing felt weird and lifeless. Whatever it was supposed to hit us with just didn’t land.

The disappointment stung more because expectations were high. On paper, this had everything — yet the execution felt flat. Casting an old man as the “pop star” centerpiece was a choice that made no sense, and it undercut the story completely. With the right lead, maybe it would’ve had something. As it stands, it was a poor showing.
