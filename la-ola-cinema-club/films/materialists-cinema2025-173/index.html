Love, lies, and luxury — romance dipped in champagne bubbles.

Romantic comedies have been missing from the mainstream rotation, and Materialist makes a case for why they still belong. Dakota Johnson has basically taken the genre on her shoulders, keeping the tradition alive with a mix of charm, wit, and modern absurdity. It leans into that glossy “slice of rich people life,” full of yachts, penthouses, and drama that’s entertaining precisely because of how ridiculous it feels. In a world where everyone is chasing “what matters,” the movie cheekily asks: do you even know what that is?

It plays like Hitch with a gender flip, wrapped in a toxic love triangle that somehow still manages to be funny and weirdly sweet. The characters make questionable decisions, but the cast sells it — Dakota Johnson in particular feels magnetic, balancing surface-level confidence with moments of real vulnerability. The twists are trippy and “WTF” enough to keep you watching, even when the story itself doesn’t dig too deep. You’ll laugh, you’ll cringe, and you might roll your eyes, but you’ll also walk away oddly satisfied.
