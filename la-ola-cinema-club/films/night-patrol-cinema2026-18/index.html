“Shady cops, a playful twist, and more fun than expected”

Night Patrol looks like it’s heading down a familiar road: cops, gang violence, and a situation you can already imagine ending badly. Then it takes a turn that makes the whole thing more playful—and more entertaining—than the trailer suggested.

That surprise does a lot for the movie. There’s real-world ugliness underneath the setup, but the film twists it into something stranger, keeping you guessing about what might happen next. Going in expecting a straightforward cop movie makes that change of direction especially enjoyable.

The pacing doesn’t always keep up with the chaos. Some stretches drag, and the cheesiness can be hard to miss. Still, the performances bring enough fun and playfulness to keep it from becoming a dull watch. It has slow parts without losing all its energy.

Justin Long’s character is harder to get a read on. He seems positioned as a good cop while continuing to do shady shit, and the movie doesn’t always make that contradiction convincing. Is he conflicted? Trying to do better? Just another bad guy? There’s room for that uncertainty to be interesting, but here it can feel more confusing than compelling.

What carries Night Patrol is its willingness to take the familiar setup somewhere unexpected. Messy in places, uneven in its momentum, but entertaining enough to make the surprise worthwhile. More fun than expected, even when it doesn’t quite make sense.
