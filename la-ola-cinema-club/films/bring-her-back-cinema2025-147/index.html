High expectations crash into chaos — messy, nasty, unpredictable.

The hype was real for this one. Coming from the directors of Talk to Me, expectations were sky-high, especially after how strong their debut landed in the horror space. They’ve been branded as the new faces of elevated, grotesque horror — so naturally, Bring Her Back was supposed to be their victory lap. Instead, it sparked one of those “love it or hate it” conversations. Was it pushing boundaries, or just pushing buttons?

For me, it leaned into the latter. The concept, zoomed out, is strong: grief, morality, the impossible choices wrapped in horror fantasy. On paper, it should hit. But the execution was messy — gross, gory, sometimes just plain nasty in ways that weren’t scary so much as off-putting. The story was hard to follow, the weirdness sometimes overwhelmed the weight of the emotions, and instead of leaving the theater shaken in the best way, I left just… annoyed. Maybe it was my own high expectations, maybe it’s a rewatch that changes everything, but my gut reaction was straight-up hate.

Still, I can admit it’s not all trash. There’s an unpredictability here that works. You don’t know where the story’s headed, the characters are bizarrely compelling, and the directors clearly have a knack for twisting the familiar into the grotesque. The problem is the balance — for every smart idea, there’s a moment that feels indulgent or just there to gross you out. I can respect the swing, but it wasn’t the kind of horror I wanted.
