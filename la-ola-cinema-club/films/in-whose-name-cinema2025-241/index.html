Kanye bares it all, but the edit can’t match the chaos.

Kanye West is one of those figures who feels impossible to escape — whether you love him, hate him, or simply feel exhausted by him. Every few years another documentary drops, trying to capture the myth, the madness, the genius, or the implosion. In Whose Name is his latest cinematic mirror, arriving in an era where the lines between performance, breakdown, and cultural commentary blur more than ever. It’s not just about Kanye as a man, but about how we consume him, interpret him, and project onto him.

On the surface, the film doesn’t feel like much more than a recap. We’ve seen many of these clips before — TMZ meltdowns, Instagram rants, messy headlines — so at times it plays like a stitched-together highlight reel. But then, when the camera lingers long enough, there are flashes of something raw. His rants shift from nonsense into brutal truths about control, family, and exploitation. He names the ways he’s boxed in, then doubles down by being uncontainable, making himself the chaos no one else can own.

It’s powerful and frustrating at the same time. You can’t deny the honesty in moments where he’s calling out how his kids are treated, or how he’s reduced to a caricature. At the same time, the editing is rough — almost like the filmmaker both nailed it and botched it, depending on the scene. What emerges is a portrait of Kanye as both martyr and manipulator, Yeezus and man, a figure who wears contradiction as a badge.

It doesn’t change how you feel about him. If you were already out, you’ll stay out. If you still believe there’s a misunderstood prophet buried inside, you’ll find fuel here. But what the doc does succeed at is showing that he’s willing to put his flaws and fury on screen, and that willingness to self-immolate is what makes Kanye endlessly polarizing, endlessly watchable, and endlessly exhausting.
