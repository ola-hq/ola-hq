A movie that exists… but never justifies why.

Ella McCay is one of those films where you finish it and immediately ask, who was this actually for? It’s a politics-and-scandal story built around pressure, optics, and public scrutiny, but none of it ever feels urgent, revealing, or especially relatable unless you already live in that world.

The movie isn’t offensively bad, it’s just baffling. It moves along, has recognizable faces, and technically works scene to scene, but nothing sticks. The problems at the center of the story feel oddly small for how seriously they’re treated, and the stakes never rise enough to pull you in emotionally. It’s the kind of movie you sit through without leaving, then never think about again.

The cast is stacked — Woody Harrelson, Jamie Lee Curtis, Kumail Nanjiani — which almost makes it more confusing. There are moments where you can tell talented people are trying, but the material gives them very little to work with. Instead of sharpening the story, the performances just highlight how thin it all feels.

Tonally, it lands in an awkward middle space. It wants to feel important, but never earns it. It wants to say something about power, image, and personal fallout, but the issues presented rarely feel consequential outside of this very specific bubble.

By the end, there’s no emotional hook, no lingering idea, and no reason to revisit it. Not disastrous — just empty.
