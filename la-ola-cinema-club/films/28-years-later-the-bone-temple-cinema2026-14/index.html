28 Years Later: The Bone Temple

“A nightmare with an unexpected pulse of humanity”

Zombie horror can offer plenty of brutality. Finding something beautiful inside it is harder. The Bone Temple does both, delivering a continuation that feels unpredictable, disturbing, and surprisingly full of life.

Two stories unfold and collide without feeling like another familiar trip through the same world. There are moments when the characters become so absorbing that you almost forget you’re watching a zombie movie. Then something deeply unsettling happens, or something makes you laugh, and you remember how strange this whole experience is. Hilarious and disturbing is a difficult combination to pull off. This one gets there.

Ralph Fiennes is the standout. The doctor’s connection with Samson gives the film an unexpected tenderness, making room for something beyond the threat of another monster. Those scenes were among the most compelling parts of the movie—strange, beautiful, and genuinely exciting to watch.

The connection between the younger characters gives the chaos another emotional anchor. There’s something worth caring about in how they find each other and offer protection inside such an ugly world. Add the gorgeous cinematography, striking shots, and an ending that leaves you buzzing, and the whole thing feels alive with possibility.

Holy fuck, this was a fantastic surprise. It left us wanting to watch it again and excited to see where this world goes next.
