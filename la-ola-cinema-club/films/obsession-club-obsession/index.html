“Getting what you want has never felt this uncomfortable”

Obsession takes a romantic wish and follows it somewhere deeply uncomfortable. Wanting someone to love you sounds simple until the movie starts questioning what that love would look like without limits, distance, or a way to turn it off.

The acting is what makes it work. Inde Navarrette carries the heaviest material, making the situation unsettling without pushing it into something we wanted to turn away from. Michael Johnston helps sell the awkwardness on the other side. You believe the people enough to stay invested when everything around them starts feeling completely fucked.

What makes it especially trippy is how extreme behavior can still have something recognizable inside it. The movie stretches impatience, desperation, and the moments people lose control into a state somebody can’t easily escape. That’s disturbing to watch. So is the sense that a person is still in there, struggling against what’s happening.

The humor lands in that same uncomfortable space. It isn’t always trying to tell a joke; sometimes the situation is so awkward and wrong that laughing is the reaction that comes out. Then the horror hits, and the whole thing tightens again.

It keeps you curious about how this could possibly work out, without making the graphic moments feel overdone. Engaging, layered, and carried by performances that make the nightmare feel much too close.
