“The future looks fucked. The present keeps checking its phone”

Good Luck, Have Fun, Don’t Die takes our relationship with technology to a ridiculous extreme, but the uncomfortable part is how much already feels familiar. The phones, the escape into virtual worlds, the promise of AI—it’s a satirical nightmare with enough truth underneath the chaos to make the jokes sting.

It’s also really fucking entertaining. The characters are interesting, the lead is easy to root for, and the story keeps finding ways to catch you off guard. For a movie dealing with such bleak possibilities, it has enough humor and unpredictability to keep the experience playful rather than crushing.

The technology itself isn’t the whole problem. There’s something exciting about tools that could connect people or open experiences they couldn’t otherwise access. The ugliness comes from what happens when those possibilities find a different purpose. Somewhere between convenience and escape, the question becomes: what are we giving up, and what are we so desperate to get away from?

That’s where it starts feeling like a modern-day Idiocracy. You laugh at the exaggeration, recognize pieces of your own world, then leave the theater and reach for your phone. The movie can point at the problem, but recognizing yourself in the joke doesn’t necessarily change anything. That’s a weird feeling to carry out of something this fun.

Not every piece lands. Some of the group gets much more attention than the rest, leaving other characters feeling less explored. The later stretch also pushes the silliness and drama further than it needs to. Still, the likable lead, surprising turns, and jokes worth catching keep it engaging.

Funny, strange, and full of ideas—an entertaining watch with an uncomfortable thought underneath it: we might already be living inside part of the punchline.
