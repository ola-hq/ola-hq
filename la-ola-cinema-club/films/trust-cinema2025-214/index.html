Betrayal hits hardest when it hides behind fame.

Trust sets itself up as a chilling industry parable — the story of a young celebrity taken advantage of by her senior co-host, manipulated into pregnancy, and then pushed to the edge as he plots to erase her. It’s a premise with weight, one that echoes the real-world whispers and headlines about abuse, coercion, and silencing women in positions of vulnerability. In that sense, it matters: these stories aren’t just fiction, they’re reflections of how power gets weaponized in entertainment and beyond.

But while the concept is potent, the execution doesn’t land as sharply as it should. Performances wobble — the lead actress shows flashes of strength and believability, but the film doesn’t give her enough to elevate the material. At times, it feels like a wasted opportunity: more soap opera than psychological thriller, more cliché than cautionary tale.

There are moments that sting, particularly when the parallels to real industry abuse feel uncomfortably plausible, and Sophie Turner adds depth where she can. Still, the overall result leans more disappointing than devastating — a story that should have left scars but instead fades too quickly.
