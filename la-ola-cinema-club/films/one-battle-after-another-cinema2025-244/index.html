A man in over his head, but never out.

In a year where immigration dominates headlines and activism gets painted in extremes, this film lands like a mirror held up to the chaos. On the surface, it plays like a slick action-thriller — DiCaprio running scared, wide shots of men and cars squaring off, explosions and shootouts. But underneath, it’s about community, rebellion, and the messy truth of good intentions colliding with bad outcomes. These are characters who start by feeding, protecting, and uplifting each other, only to end up blowing up buildings, robbing to fund their cause, and crossing lines they never meant to cross. That tension — good people forced into bad acts — gives the movie a weight that hits differently in today’s cultural climate.

The film even dips into satirical absurdity with something like Operation Christmas (or “Christmas Adventures,” depending on how you hear it). You can’t quite tell if the filmmakers leaned too hard into parody, making these groups look dumb, or if it’s actually close to real history, which would make it chilling. Either way, it lingers. The irony is that the trailer and marketing hardly hinted at this, probably to keep mainstream audiences focused on the action. But once you’re in it, you realize this is very much a movie about borders, power, and rebellion.

And then there are the performances. Leo nails the “decent guy in over his head,” terrified but stubbornly trying to keep his humanity intact while doing what survival demands. The fractured family dynamic adds bite: the wife’s choices cut deep, but the daughter steals the show. She’s straight-up badass — not because anyone pushed her into it, but because her parents lived it and handed her the tools. She chose to pick them up, and that choice gives her arc real power. For a debut (or near-debut), it’s a standout performance.

The real-time rating experience said a lot: bouncing between three and four, never sliding down, but never hitting that five. And maybe the reason it didn’t break through is simple — as much as we respected the characters, we didn’t feel them. Not the mom, not Leo, and barely even the daughter.
