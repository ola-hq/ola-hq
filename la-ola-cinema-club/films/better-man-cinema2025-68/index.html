A love story to oneself, to life, and to the music.

Better Man is one of those just-makes-you-feel movies. I first caught it at AMC Unseen, but I rewatched it today just to put this review together — and it still hit just as hard. It’s a love story to oneself, a love story to life. Robbie Williams made this biopic about his journey, and it’s powerful, raw, and really well done. The CGI was surprisingly strong, the money on screen shows, and it felt like a true passion project.

What stood out most was how fresh and original it felt. It didn’t play like a typical musical or biopic. It was captivating, magical, even mystic at times — and crazy how many details were so spot-on they added to the weight of the story. The drug use was portrayed with such raw honesty it became one of the hardest but most powerful parts of the film. It’s a rollercoaster, emotional and surreal, but always compelling.

Yes, there are strange choices — the monkey bit is a hurdle to get past — but once you’re through it, the movie finds its rhythm. The songs, the performances, and the emotional payoff at the end make it more than worth it. Even if you’re not familiar with Robbie’s career, the movie captures his rise to fame and the toll it took in a way that’s both heartfelt and cinematic.
