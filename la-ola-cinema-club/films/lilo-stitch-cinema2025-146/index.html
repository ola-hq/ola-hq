Nostalgia carries it halfway, but the magic feels clipped.

Live-action remakes are always tricky — too faithful, and you risk being stale; too different, and you alienate the diehards. Disney keeps mining its animated vault, and Stitch was always going to be a tough one. The cultural footprint is massive, the Elvis needle-drops iconic, and the Ohana message timeless. So when this dropped, it mattered because it wasn’t just another remake — it was Disney trying to bottle lightning twice, during a strike year no less.

And that strike context shows. The movie feels oddly stiff, like the actors weren’t allowed to breathe or improv, leaving performances that don’t always land. Some casting choices felt off, and key beats were either missing or rushed. Elvis barely gets his due, which is wild considering how much he anchored the original vibe. But even with those gaps, there are still moments that work — the ocean, the chaos, the heartstrings of family sticking together. You feel flashes of what made the cartoon a classic.

Nostalgia really does a lot of the heavy lifting here. It’s fun to see certain scenes come to life in live-action, and Stitch remains a cultural juggernaut who still hits kids and adults alike. The film is uneven, yes, but it manages to thread enough of the original spirit while setting up room for sequels. If nothing else, it’s proof that Stitch is here to stay.
