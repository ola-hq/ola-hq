When love is all you have left, vengeance becomes the only mission.

The Amateur pushes into the tragic love-story lane wrapped in espionage — less about saving the world, more about a man who’s lost everything and is willing to burn it all down. Directed by James Hawes and starring Rami Malek, Rachel Brosnahan, Laurence Fishburne, and more, it sits in that category of ordinary people turned unstoppable by grief. It matters because grief is a powerful fuel, and this movie uses the spy-ops framework to ask: what do you do when your love is your last reason to keep going. ￼

I really enjoyed this one. Watching Charlie, clearly out of his depth, fight tooth and nail because he had nothing left to lose was gripping. The acting sold it — you felt for him, even as he tore everyone down. It scratched the same itch as that movie where Gerard Butler run circles around the justice system from a jail cell.

It’s a fine movie. Good acting, solid tension, well put together. It’s just not the kind of spy thriller I naturally gravitate toward — the emotional moments hit harder for some than others, and at times it leaned into tropes. Still, for what it tries to do, it mostly works.
