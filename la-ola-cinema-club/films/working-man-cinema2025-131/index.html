Jason Statham clocks in again, but this shift feels familiar.

Jason Statham has built his career on reliable action flicks — gritty one-liners, bruising fight scenes, and enough plot just to tie together the chaos. Originally announced as Levon’s Trade, A Working Man sticks to that familiar Statham formula: action, grit, and just enough story to keep it moving. Directed by David Ayer, co-written with Sylvester Stallone, it stars Statham alongside Michael Peña and David Harbour. It matters because fans of that style want the punch-to-the-face moments, and this delivers — even if it doesn’t surprise.

This movie wasn’t terrible. The action delivers a few cool sequences, and his blind friend adds a cheeky dynamic that was fun. It wasn’t hard to sit through, but it’s not something I’d rave about either. The ending especially disappointed — we expected a badass moment from the female lead that never came. So yeah, a generous three.

It’s a basic Statham action movie that hits the beats but doesn’t do much more. The storyline worked in places, but things felt familiar — mission, rescue, hero moves. By the time the credits rolled, it felt like just another paycheck flick: good for fans, but not groundbreaking.
