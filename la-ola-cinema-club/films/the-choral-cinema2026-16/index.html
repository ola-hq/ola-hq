“Finding a voice while the world prepares to take it away”

There’s something quietly sad about people building a life together while a war keeps pulling it apart. The Choral finds beauty, humor, and warmth in that space, following a group whose shared purpose becomes harder to hold onto as the world changes around them.

The pleasure is often in the people. The central character’s personality and the way he runs things give the film energy, while the younger characters and those connected to the army bring entertaining moments of their own. The bikes, outfits, and sets make the period setting enjoyable to spend time in, with different lives unfolding around the choir.

Watching the group improve gives the story a satisfying progression. The singing can be beautiful, and it avoids the cheesiness that sometimes makes musicals difficult to settle into. But those performances also slow the film down. There’s a lot to appreciate without every musical stretch being equally absorbing, and the running time starts to make itself felt.

What gives the movie weight is the gradual loss of the people bringing those voices together. The story comes full circle in a way that feels sad rather than reassuring. People try to be patriotic and keep something meaningful alive, while having very little say in what happens to them.

That feeling—being a pawn in a much bigger game—lingers beyond the music. A worthwhile watch with heartfelt and funny moments, even if its slower passages keep it from landing more strongly.
