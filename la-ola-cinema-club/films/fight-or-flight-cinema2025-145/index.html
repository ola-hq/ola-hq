High-altitude chaos, wild action, and just enough turbulence to entertain.

Airplane thrillers have their own subgenre DNA — tight quarters, limited escape, and the pressure cooker of strangers trapped together. Fight or Flight leans into that formula but dresses it up with Josh Hartnett (yep, he’s back) and a mix of martial arts spectacle with Hollywood polish. It matters because it scratches the itch for pure action escapism at a time when audiences are still chasing high-concept blockbusters that don’t ask too much from the brain.

The movie is pretty much what you’d expect: nonstop brawls, creative set pieces, and a few outlandish “what the fuck” moments that feel pulled out of a kung fu wire-fu playbook. It’s entertaining in that airplane-movie way — not groundbreaking, but fun enough to kill a couple hours. Hartnett brings enough charm to make it watchable, and while the plot isn’t reinventing anything, it finds small ways to keep things fresh. By setting everything inside the plane, the filmmakers leaned hard on the acting and close-quarters choreography, which actually works in the movie’s favor.

Not great, not terrible — just solid, popcorn-friendly action that’s best enjoyed with your tray table locked and seat back upright.
